Los Monteros, from Río Real to Las Adelfas

Los Monteros is an established beachside urbanisation immediately east of Marbella town, lying between the coast road and the sand. Its beach, Las Adelfas, flew a Blue Flag in 2026, and an eighteen-hole Javier Arana course from 1965 sits behind it. Plots are large and the layout has not changed in decades.

Los Monteros belongs to the first generation of planned beachside living east of Marbella town, and it still reads that way: wide streets, mature planting, low buildings and plots sized before land became scarce. Its beach is registered by the municipality as Playa Las Adelfas, at Calle Monteros, and it carries both a Blue Flag and the Q de Calidad quality mark. Behind the urbanisation, the Río Real course, established in 1965 to a Javier Arana design, eighteen holes at par 72, separates the housing from the hills. Between the two runs the old coast road.

The stock is mostly detached houses on generous plots, with a smaller number of low-rise apartment communities closer to the sand, and townhouse rows filling the gaps. Very little of it is new. Buildings sit low, gardens are old enough to have proper shade, and the boundary walls are established rather than freshly rendered. That combination produces a specific kind of market: buyers are usually choosing between a house that needs a full renovation and one that has already had one, and the difference between those two is the whole negotiation.

Life here is quieter than the town and busier than a hillside. The beach is used all year by residents, with chiringuitos such as El Kiosko Kite Surf and El Mangaleta on the sand, and the coastal path is progressively linking this stretch to its neighbours as the Senda del Litoral is completed section by section. What the urbanisation does not have is a centre, no square, no shops of consequence, so daily errands go into Marbella, four minutes west. Paula's read is that this is a place people move to twice: once for the beach, once for the trees.

The prevailing type is the low, wide villa on a flat or gently sloping plot, built from the 1960s onwards in the classical coastal manner: white render, tiled roofs, deep porches, gardens laid out for shade rather than display. Apartment building exists but stays low and sits closer to the sand, usually as small communities with mature planting rather than towers. Since 2000 a steady programme of demolition and rebuild has introduced flat-roofed contemporary houses on the same generous plots, so two adjacent addresses can be sixty years apart in specification and identical in footprint.

The beach is the amenity and it works out of season, which is the argument for this address over a hillside one. Las Adelfas has chiringuitos on it and residents on it in winter, and the coastal path is being extended along this shoreline in stages. Golf is immediately behind, on a course laid out in 1965. What is missing is a centre: there are no meaningful shops, so the supermarket, the pharmacy and the school run all go west into Marbella. Traffic is a town problem here, not an urbanisation one.

It suits buyers who want a beach they will actually use in November, a large plot near the town rather than far from it, and a neighbourhood where the trees came first. It suits renovators with patience. It does not suit anyone who wants shops, bars or a school within walking distance, a buyer looking for a brand-new gated development with a concierge, or someone who needs a large modern house immediately: most of what is here will need work before it looks the way a magazine promised.

What moves the price here

Los Monteros sits high on the Marbella ladder: below Golden Mile, but above Puerto Banús and well above Elviria further east. What supports that position is beach frontage combined with plot sizes the town centre cannot offer, in an area that is finished and cannot expand. The spread within the urbanisation is wide, because a renovated house and an original one of the same size are two different propositions.

Distance to the sand, plot size, and whether the house has been rebuilt or simply maintained. After that: the age of the pool and services, the maturity of the garden, and how much of the plot is usable rather than boundary.

Questions about Los Monteros

Which beach belongs to Los Monteros, and is it any good?

Playa Las Adelfas, registered by the Ayuntamiento at Calle Monteros. It holds both a Blue Flag and the Q de Calidad, and the 2026 award list names it as Los Monteros (Adelfas). In practice it is a broad, serviced beach with chiringuitos including El Kiosko Kite Surf and El Mangaleta, used by residents outside the summer as well as in it. Both quality marks are reviewed each season rather than granted once.

↗

How old is the golf course behind Los Monteros?

It dates from 1965 and was designed by Javier Arana, which places it among the earliest courses on this part of the coast. Río Real plays eighteen holes at par 72 and sits directly behind the urbanisation, between the housing and the hills. For a buyer that matters in two ways: mature trees and an established layout on one side of the property, and a course boundary that is not going to be redeveloped on the other.

↗

Are there shops within walking distance in Los Monteros?

Not really, and that is the honest trade for the plot sizes. The urbanisation was laid out as housing between a road and a beach, without a commercial centre, so a supermarket, a pharmacy and a school all mean driving west into Marbella town: a short journey, but a daily one. What is within walking distance is the sand, the chiringuitos on it and, for members, the golf behind. Buyers who want a walkable high street should look at the town itself.

Is buying a house here usually a renovation project?

More often than not. The urbanisation is one of the older planned beachside areas east of the town, so a large share of the villas were built decades ago on plots that are now hard to replicate. The result is a market in which the real choice is between an original house and one that has already been rebuilt. Because Marbella's 2010 general plan was annulled in 2015 and the 1986 plan applies again, the licence history of any earlier work needs tracing first.

↗

Can you walk along the coast from Los Monteros?

Increasingly, but not yet everywhere. The Senda del Litoral is a coastal route of roughly 180 kilometres from Manilva to Nerja, assembled in 167 separate sections from existing promenades, cycle paths and new connecting links, and it is completed unevenly along this shore. In practice some stretches join up and others end at a river mouth or a private boundary. Walk the section in front of a specific property before assuming an unbroken promenade in either direction.
