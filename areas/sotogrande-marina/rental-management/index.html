Sotogrande Marina · Rental management

Letting on the Sotogrande quays starts with your neighbours' vote

A marina apartment is the one Sotogrande home a guest can use without a car, which is what makes it lettable. But the estate is a set of apartment blocks, so the decision is not yours alone: since April 2025 your comunidad de propietarios must approve tourist letting before the Andalucían registration is worth pursuing.

Guests come to the quays for a reason that is easy to describe and hard to replicate elsewhere on the estate: they can arrive, park once and not touch the car again. Bread, a coffee, a restaurant, a boat and the Sunday market along the Ribera del Marlín are all on the same walk. Families who have rented a villa in Sotogrande Alto once and spent the week driving tend to book here the following year. That is the whole letting proposition, and it means the flats that let best are not necessarily the ones with the best water view: they are the ones with a lift, storage and a parking space close to the door.

The constraint is the building itself. Every flat here sits in a comunidad de propietarios, and Spanish horizontal property law now gives that community real control: an owner must obtain its express prior approval before running a tourist let, and the community may limit or condition tourist letting by a three-fifths vote of owners representing three-fifths of the quotas. Read the minutes before you buy a letting flat and before you renew a management plan. On these quays, where blocks are small and neighbours are permanent residents rather than absent owners, that vote is a live question rather than a formality.

The season on the quays, and the licence position in San Roque

The season is short and legible. The estate's population multiplies several times over its winter figure in high summer, 2,743 residents at the 2020 census against an estimated 12,500 in season, and the quays are where that surge is most visible, because they are where everyone eats. Once the Spanish school year restarts the flow stops quickly. Any letting plan that assumes shoulder-season demand comparable to Fuengirola is planning for a different stretch of coast.

The regulatory picture, as at 26 August 2026, has three layers and they are often confused. Regionally, a vivienda con fines turísticos is declared by declaración responsable and entered in the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía under Decreto 28/2016, with the resulting code shown in advertising. Nationally, the Ley de Propiedad Horizontal now requires the community's express prior approval for tourist letting and lets a community limit or condition it by a three-fifths vote. Municipally, the Ayuntamiento de San Roque is the authority, and municipal positions along this coast have changed more than once in recent years. Paula's position is unchanged and deliberately conservative: confirm the community's minuted decision and the municipality's current position in writing before you buy on the strength of a letting plan, and never rely on what applied last season.

How this runs, step by step

  1. Step 1

    Read the minutes before anything else

    Specific to San Roque

    Start with the last three sets of comunidad minutes for your block, not with a booking platform. As at 26 August 2026, Spanish law requires the community's express prior approval before a home is let to tourists, and permits the community to limit or condition it by a three-fifths vote. If that vote has gone against, nothing downstream matters.

    ↗

  2. Step 2

    Register the home in Andalucía

    A vivienda con fines turísticos in Andalucía is declared to the regional tourism authority by declaración responsable and entered in the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía under Decreto 28/2016. The code that comes back has to appear in your advertising. This is the regional layer, and it is separate from anything the municipality or your community decides.

    ↗

  3. Step 3

    Confirm the municipal position in writing

    Specific to San Roque

    Municipal positions on tourist letting move, and they differ from one town hall to the next along this coast. The property sits in San Roque, whose Valle del Guadiaro district office is in Guadiaro rather than in San Roque town. Ask there, in writing, what applies on the date you are acting, and treat any answer more than a few months old as out of date.

  4. Step 4

    Set the flat up for arrivals on foot

    Specific to San Roque

    Guests come here to leave the car. That makes the parking space, the lift, the luggage route from the space to the door and secure storage for bikes or paddleboards more valuable than a second bathroom. Add a written note on where the Sunday market runs and which restaurants below the block stay open out of season, because guests who find a shuttered quay in February do not rebook.

  5. Step 5

    Plan for a two-part year

    Specific to San Roque

    Run the calendar as two products. The short-let weeks are concentrated around the polo and the boat traffic; outside them, a longer tenancy taken by someone working across the Gibraltar frontier is usually worth more than an empty flat waiting for a weekend booking. Switching between the two mid-year is where most owners lose money on cleaning, changeovers and voids.

  6. Step 6

    Keep the paperwork current

    Registration is not a one-off. Guest registration duties, the display of the registry code in every advertisement and any change to the home's capacity or ownership all need updating, and the rules around short-term letting have moved repeatedly in recent years. Diary an annual review with the lawyer rather than assuming a code issued once stays correct forever.

A flat on these quays earns its best weeks when the polo and the boat traffic are here, and very little in the weeks either side. Because the estate empties in winter, the calendar behind that peak is usually filled by a longer tenancy rather than by weekend bookings. Against that sit the running costs an owner cannot avoid: the block's community charge, the estate conservation levy, the IBI set by San Roque, and management, cleaning and changeover costs that scale with the number of short stays rather than with the income. Paula models a specific block before any of this is committed to.

Questions about this in Sotogrande Marina

Can my block stop me letting a marina flat short-term?

Yes, and this changed recently. As at 26 August 2026, an owner needs the community's express prior approval before running a tourist let, and the community may limit or condition tourist letting by a vote of three-fifths of owners representing three-fifths of the quotas. On these quays the blocks are small and a meaningful share of owners live here year-round, so the vote is genuinely contested in some buildings and settled in others. Read the minutes before you rely on income.

↗

Do guests here really manage without a car?

In the marina, yes, and it is the reason bookings come back. Shops, restaurants, the quays and the Sunday market along the Ribera del Marlín are all within the same walk, and a boat trip starts at the end of it. Elsewhere on the estate a car is required for a litre of milk, which is exactly why families who rented in the upper sectors one year often book down here the next. Out of season the picture thins, so tell guests honestly what is open in February.

Is a long tenancy better than short lets on the quays?

For many owners here, yes for most of the year. The short-let peak is narrow and concentrated around the summer weeks, while the rest of the calendar on these quays is carried by people working across the Gibraltar frontier who want a twelve-month agreement. A hybrid, peak weeks short, the remainder on a longer contract, is workable but needs the community's position settled first, and it costs more in changeovers than owners expect. Decide the model before furnishing.
