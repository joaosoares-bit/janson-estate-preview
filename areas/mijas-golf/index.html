Mijas Golf: the valley of Los Lagos and Los Olivos

Mijas Golf is a residential golf valley inland of Fuengirola, inside Mijas municipality on the Málaga coast. It is organised around two eighteen-hole courses: Los Lagos, opened in 1976 to a Robert Trent Jones Senior design, and Los Olivos, opened in 1984 and remodelled at the turn of the century.

The valley sits behind Fuengirola, between the coastal build-up and the wall of the Sierra de Mijas, and it exists because of the golf. Los Lagos came first, opened in 1976 across roughly fifty-two hectares of gently undulating ground with nine lakes that bring water into play on eleven of the eighteen holes. Los Olivos followed in 1984 on tighter, hillier terrain of about forty hectares, with smaller and more sloping greens and heavy bunkering, and it was remodelled in the 2000 to 2001 season. Housing arrived around the fairways rather than the other way round.

What is for sale is mostly low-rise: apartment and townhouse communities backing onto the courses, a smaller number of detached villas on individual plots, and a scattering of older houses that predate the second course. The best of it faces a fairway with the sierra behind; the least considered backs onto a service road. Because the valley filled in over three decades, specification varies wildly between neighbouring communities: underfloor heating and a lift in one, a single-glazed 1980s townhouse with a spiral stair in the next.

Life here is quiet and entirely car-based. There is no town centre, no promenade and no parade of shops beyond what the clubhouses and a few local bars provide; Fuengirola supplies the supermarkets, the schools, the station and the beach, and Mijas Pueblo the Sunday lunch. That is the caveat and it is a real one: a resident who does not play golf can find the valley very still by January. The market read is that demand here is specific rather than broad: buyers arrive because of the two courses, and they price the address accordingly.

The valley reads as three building generations. Late 1970s and 1980s townhouses in a plain Andalusian idiom, white render, tiled roofs, small windows, spiral stairs to a solarium, sit closest to the older course. The 1990s brought larger apartment communities with communal pools, gardens and, in the better schemes, garage levels. The 2000s added blocks with lifts, wider glazing and terraces designed as outdoor rooms. Detached villas are scattered rather than gathered, and the plots are generous by coastal standards because land here was cheaper than frontline land. Renovation stock is abundant and often untouched since first sale.

In February the two clubhouses are the social centre and very nearly the only one: coffee after nine holes, a menu at lunch, and quiet by nine in the evening. Residents drive down to Fuengirola for the supermarket, the health centre and the train, and up to Mijas Pueblo for a Sunday table with a view. The school run leaves the valley entirely. There are no pavements to speak of between communities, so walking means walking the course perimeter roads. The sound is sprinklers, birds and the occasional buggy.

It suits golfers who want to walk to a first tee, buyers who prefer a green outlook to a sea view, and owners who are content to drive for everything else. Retired couples and semi-retired second-home owners make up most of the resident pool. It does not suit anyone who wants a village on the doorstep, anyone relying on public transport, or a family that wants children to reach friends, shops and a beach without being driven there.

What moves the price here

Mijas Golf sits high on the Mijas ladder, below La Cala de Mijas and above Calahonda, Riviera del Sol and Mijas Pueblo. It is the unusual case of an inland address holding its own against coastal ones, and the reason is the two courses rather than the setting. Against the municipality's inland districts the gap widens sharply, because those are resident markets and this is a golf market.

Which course a property fronts and whether the outlook is fairway or service road, the age and specification of the community, plot size on the detached houses, garage provision, and how far the sierra shadow falls across the garden in midwinter.

Questions about Mijas Golf

How do Los Lagos and Los Olivos compare for a resident?

Los Lagos is the older and more open of the two, laid out in 1976 across roughly fifty-two hectares of gently undulating ground with nine lakes that put water in play on eleven holes. Los Olivos, opened in 1984 on about forty hectares, is shorter, tighter and hillier, with smaller sloping greens, heavy bunkering and olive trees along the corridors; it was remodelled in the 2000 to 2001 season. For a buyer the practical difference is the housing beside each, which differs in age and in community standard.

↗

Does buying a house at Mijas Golf include club membership?

No. The courses and the housing are separate concerns, and a title deed carries no playing right at either layout. Residents book green fees or take up whatever membership the club offers at the time, and both change independently of the property market. Anyone buying for the golf should establish what regular play will actually involve before agreeing a price, because a fairway view is a view, not a subscription, and it is not unusual for owners to play elsewhere in the municipality.

How far is Mijas Golf from Fuengirola and the beach?

Close enough to be routine and far enough that you will always drive it. The valley lies immediately inland of Fuengirola, so the supermarket, the health centre, the railway station and the seafront are a short journey down the hill rather than a walk. There is no useful bus for the way residents actually live. In practice most households here keep two cars, or accept that one person's day is shaped around the other's use of the only one.

Is a fairway-front property a good idea at Mijas Golf?

Usually yes, with two things to check. Los Lagos carries nine lakes and water in play on eleven holes, so ground close to a lake edge or a low fairway should be looked at for drainage after heavy autumn rain. The second is exposure: a house on the line of play takes stray balls, and the older communities were laid out before netting became normal. A plot set slightly back, still with the open outlook, is often the better long-term buy.

↗

Is Mijas Golf a sensible place to live all year?

For a golfer, comfortably. For anyone else, it depends on tolerance for stillness. The valley has no commercial centre of its own, so winter evenings are quiet and social life concentrates on the two clubhouses. Residents who do not play tend to build their week around Fuengirola instead, which works but means the address is a base rather than a neighbourhood. Spend a February afternoon here before deciding, not an August one, because the two are barely the same place.
