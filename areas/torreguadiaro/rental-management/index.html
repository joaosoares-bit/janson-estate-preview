Letting in Torreguadiaro, where changeover is the problem

A guest in Torreguadiaro needs no car once they arrive: the sand, the chiringuitos, the bakery and the walk to Sotogrande's port are all inside ten minutes. The owner's problem is the opposite one. In a village two streets deep, the summer changeover has nowhere to stand, and that decides how a flat is run.

San Roque

Plan the changeover before the listing, because in this village it is the whole operation. From June to September the streets between the paseo and the A-7 are parked solid, and there is no service road behind the front-line blocks to work from. Establish where a cleaning vehicle can legally stop, how long linen can sit on a pavement, whether the block has a lift and at which end of the building it sits, and who else has a key. Guests can arrive without a car if they want to, the M-240 runs Estepona to La Línea and Línea 2, operated by Autobuses Esteban, links San Roque town centre with the village, but the people servicing the flat cannot, and that asymmetry is what makes or breaks a summer here.

The competition is closer than owners expect. This village never turned into a resort, and the small hotels and hostales that grew up alongside the fishermen's houses are still trading on the same two streets, with a reception, a breakfast and a rate that moves in September. A flat does not win against that on price for a two-night stay. It wins on a week or more: a kitchen, separate bedrooms, a terrace over the bay, and a stay long enough that the bakery and the beach kiosk become routine. Paula's read is that owners who write the listing around a stay of a week or longer do consistently better here than owners chasing weekend bookings.

What the village year looks like, and who sets the rules

The year has a sharp peak and a floor that is unusually high for this corridor. From late June the paseo fills, the chiringuitos run music into the night and the front-line flats sell every week they have. Then it stops quickly. What does not stop is the village itself: a working core of bars and the bakery keep going for a resident population, so the quiet half of the year is liveable rather than shuttered. That matters commercially, because it makes a longer tenancy a genuine alternative to an empty calendar rather than a fallback nobody wants.

The rules come from three places and it is worth keeping them apart. Regionally, Decreto 28/2016 governs a vivienda con fines turísticos: declaración responsable, entry in the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía, the code in every advertisement, technical requirements for the dwelling, and an exclusion for lets of more than two continuous months to the same user. Nationally, since 3 April 2025 the community's express prior approval is required and a three-fifths vote may limit or condition the activity. Municipally, San Roque is the town hall, and it published no dedicated notice on tourist dwellings on its own site as at 27 August 2026, so the municipal answer is one to obtain in writing for a specific address, on the day you act, and never to assume from last season.

How this runs, step by step

  1. Step 1

    Solve the August changeover first

    Specific to San Roque

    Walk the route with a suitcase and a laundry bag before a single week is sold. Where does a vehicle stop, and for how long? Is the lift at the far end of the block from the street door? Where is spare linen stored, given that a village flat rarely has a garage? Fix all of that in writing with whoever cleans, then build the arrival and departure times around the answer rather than around a platform's default.

  2. Step 2

    Position the flat against the hostales

    Specific to San Roque

    Walk the two streets and price the small hotels and hostales trading on them, because they are your real competitors for a short stay. Then stop competing with them. Write the listing for a week or more and lead with what a room cannot offer: a kitchen, separate bedrooms, a washing machine, a terrace over the bay and space for a family that would otherwise need two rooms.

  3. Step 3

    Declare the dwelling to the Junta

    A vivienda con fines turísticos in Andalucía is set up by declaración responsable and entered in the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía under Decreto 28/2016, with the registration code shown in every advertisement: position as at 27 August 2026. The decree also sets technical requirements for the dwelling and excludes lets of more than two continuous months to the same user, which is the line between two quite different regimes.

    ↗

  4. Step 4

    Get the community's approval minuted

    Since 3 April 2025 an owner needs the community's express prior approval before letting to tourists, and a three-fifths vote of owners and quotas may limit or condition it: position as at 27 August 2026. Obtain the minutes for your own block and read what was actually resolved and when. A verbal assurance from a neighbour, or the fact that another flat in the building already lets, is not the same document.

    ↗

  5. Step 5

    Ask San Roque for its written position

    Specific to San Roque

    Municipal positions on tourist letting differ along this coast and are being revised. The Ayuntamiento de San Roque, at Plaza de las Constituciones s/n, published no dedicated notice on the subject on its own site as at 27 August 2026, which is neither a permission nor a prohibition. Request the position for your exact address, in writing, on the day you act, and where letting is the plan, make it a written condition of a purchase.

    ↗

  6. Step 6

    Equip the flat for a walked stay

    Specific to San Roque

    The village's argument is that a guest parks once and then walks, so equip for that. Somewhere to hang wet towels, a rack for beach things, a rinse point if the block has one, blackout at the front where the paseo stays awake, and a written note on which bakery opens early and which kiosks run in the off months. Then say plainly which stretch of the paseo the flat sits on.

A flat here earns hard in a short window and steadily in nothing else, so the honest model puts the peak weeks against a quieter alternative rather than against an optimistic year. Because a resident population stays put, a longer tenancy in the off months is a real option, which is not true of the gated addresses a few minutes away. Against the income sit the block's own charge, cleaning and management costs that scale with the number of stays, and the capital works an ageing beach block eventually calls for. Ask Paula what comparable flats on your own stretch of the paseo are actually achieving before a figure is assumed.

Questions about this in Torreguadiaro

Where will my cleaners park on a August changeover?

That is the question to answer before the flat is listed, not after the first complaint. The village runs about two streets deep between the sand and the A-7, with no service road behind the front-line blocks, and from June to September those streets are full. In practice owners solve it with a short legal stop, a trolley, a lift they have checked with luggage, and linen stored inside the flat rather than brought each time. Agree the arrangement in writing with whoever cleans, and set your arrival and departure times to suit it.

Am I competing with the hostales on these streets?

For a short stay, yes, and you will usually lose. The small hotels and hostales that grew alongside the fishermen's houses never became resorts, and they still trade on the same streets with a reception desk, a breakfast and a rate that drops once September arrives. The way round it is not to price against them. Sell the week rather than the weekend, and lead on what a room cannot supply: a kitchen, separate bedrooms, a washing machine, a terrace and room for a family that would otherwise book two.

Does the Blue Flag actually help a listing here?

It helps as evidence rather than as decoration. Playa de Torreguadiaro was among the six Blue Flags the Ayuntamiento de San Roque renewed for the 2026 season, and the award turns on water quality, services, safety and accessibility, all assessed annually. For a guest choosing between a village they have never heard of and a resort they have, that is a checkable third-party signal rather than an adjective. Name the beach and the year in the listing, and let a reader verify it themselves.
