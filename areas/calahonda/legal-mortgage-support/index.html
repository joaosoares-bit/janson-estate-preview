Calahonda · Legal & mortgage support

Four layers of law meet at a Calahonda front door

Most Spanish purchases involve three layers: national law, Andalucían tax and the municipality. A Calahonda purchase adds a fourth, because Sitio de Calahonda is maintained by a conservation entity with its own legal personality and obligatory membership, and your lawyer has to clear its position as well as the community's.

Separate the layers before you separate the fees. Purchase law, contract, deed, registration, the mortgage rules that give a borrower a statutory reflection period, is national and identical whether you buy here or in Galicia. Transfer tax and stamp duty are regional and filed with the Agencia Tributaria de Andalucía. Plusvalía municipal, the IBI rate and the planning file belong to the Ayuntamiento de Mijas, whose seat is up in the village with branch offices in Las Lagunas and La Cala. So far, ordinary. The fourth layer is what makes this address unusual, and it is the one buyers meet last, usually after the offer has been accepted.

The Entidad Urbanística de Conservación that maintains Sitio de Calahonda is an administrative body with full legal personality, its statutes approved by the Ayuntamiento and registered with the Junta de Andalucía, and every owner belongs to it by law. It is not a comunidad de propietarios and should not be treated as one. Your lawyer therefore has two arrears positions to clear rather than one, two sets of accounts to read, and two bodies to notify after completion. None of it is difficult. All of it is invisible on a standard purchase checklist written for a flat in Fuengirola, which is exactly why it goes wrong.

National, Andalucían, municipal, and the entity

The clean separation is worth holding in your head, because it tells you who to ask. National: the contract, the deed, the Registro de la Propiedad, and the mortgage protections that give a borrower ten calendar days and a free notarial act before being bound. Andalucían: transfer tax and stamp duty, filed with the Agencia Tributaria de Andalucía, at rates your lawyer confirms in writing rather than a page quotes. Municipal: plusvalía, IBI, the planning file and the certificate of applicable regime, all of them at the Ayuntamiento de Mijas, whose registry and notarial work falls in the judicial district of Fuengirola.

The fourth layer is the local one. Because the conservation entity is an administrative body rather than a private association, its charge is not simply a service bill you could argue about; the obligation attaches to ownership by law. Practically, that means a lawyer here reads two sets of accounts and clears two arrears positions, and it means a valuation is the easy part of the file rather than the hard part. In this market the valuer has real comparable trades to work from, so the risk is almost never the number. It is what has been added to a hillside house over forty years without anyone telling the Ayuntamiento.

How this runs, step by step

  1. Step 1

    Arrange the NIE and representation

    Every foreign buyer needs a NIE before a deed can be signed, and a power of attorney is worth arranging early if you are not in Spain for every step. Set both in motion when the search starts rather than when an offer is accepted, because appointment availability, not the paperwork itself, is what usually moves a completion date.

  2. Step 2

    Order the entity file too

    Specific to Mijas

    Alongside the comunidad certificate, instruct your lawyer to obtain the conservation entity's statement for the property and its current statutes. Membership is obligatory for every owner in Sitio de Calahonda and the statutes were approved by the Ayuntamiento and registered with the Junta, so this is a public-law obligation you are inheriting rather than an optional service.

    ↗

  3. Step 3

    Check the plot's planning position

    Specific to Mijas

    Mijas publishes its urbanismo procedures and issues a certificate of the planning regime applying to a specific plot. On a hillside villa with terraces, a pool or an enclosed porch added over the decades, that certificate answers whether what exists is what was permitted, before you commit rather than when you come to sell.

    ↗

  4. Step 4

    Use the statutory mortgage window

    Spanish law gives a borrower at least ten calendar days between receiving the lender's information pack and being bound, and a free notarial act in which the notary explains the terms no later than the day before signing. Use the full window to compare offers rather than treating it as a delay to be waived.

    ↗

  5. Step 5

    File the regional tax properly

    Transfer tax on a resale, or stamp duty on a new build, is filed with the Agencia Tributaria de Andalucía within the statutory window. Your lawyer confirms the current rates in writing before you commit rather than relying on a figure read somewhere online, because the rate is a regional decision that changes without warning your purchase.

    ↗

  6. Step 6

    Notify both bodies after completion

    Specific to Mijas

    After registration, your lawyer changes the IBI and utility records and notifies the comunidad. The step people forget is the conservation entity, which bills the urbanisation's shared costs annually and will keep addressing them to the previous owner until told otherwise. Send it a copy of the deed and confirm the new billing address in writing.

    ↗

Some costs are set by rule and some are yours. Notary and registry follow published tariffs, the Andalucían tax follows the declared value, and the lender's valuation is largely fixed. Negotiable are the lawyer's engagement, the survey, and the mortgage terms once you have used the statutory window to compare. Do not economise on the two things this address actually turns on: a proper reading of the entity and community files, and a planning check on anything added to a hillside house. Those two are the cheapest protection in the transaction and the two most often skipped.

Questions about this in Calahonda

Is the conservation levy legally the same as a community fee?

No, and the difference matters when something goes wrong. A comunidad de propietarios is a private arrangement between owners of one building under the Ley de Propiedad Horizontal. The conservation entity is an administrative body with its own legal personality, created under planning law and registered with the Junta de Andalucía, and membership follows ownership automatically. Treat its statement as a public-law obligation to be cleared, not as an invoice to be negotiated.

↗

Which district handles the court side of a purchase here?

Fuengirola. Mijas municipality falls in that judicial district, so anything contentious attached to a purchase, an injunction, a dispute over a boundary, an enforcement against unpaid charges, is filed there rather than in Marbella or Málaga city. It is a small practical point that becomes a large one if a transaction turns awkward, and it is worth knowing which town your lawyer will actually be travelling to.

↗

Will a lender look differently at a hillside villa here?

The valuation itself is usually straightforward, because this market trades often enough to give a valuer real comparable sales. What changes the lender's view is the file rather than the figure: an enclosed terrace, a pool or a garage added over the decades and never reconciled with the registered description will hold up an offer until it is regularised. Sort the planning position early and the mortgage tends to be the least eventful part of the purchase.
