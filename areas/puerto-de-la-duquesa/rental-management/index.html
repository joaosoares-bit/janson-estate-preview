Puerto de la Duquesa · Rental management

Letting at Duquesa, where everything happens on one quay

A Duquesa flat lets on one argument: the guest parks once and walks to everything, because the restaurants, the bars and the water are the same circuit. The counterweight sits directly below the frontline blocks, where that arcade is also the loudest thing in the building's minutes.

The operational advantage here is smaller than a beach urbanisation and that is exactly the point. A changeover at Duquesa is walked, not driven: the cleaner, the linen, the key handover and the welcome all happen along a single quay rather than between gates two kilometres apart. That compresses the cost of running a let and makes a same-day turnaround realistic in August, which is when a turnaround is worth money. It also means your guest's first impression is formed by the state of the quay outside your block rather than by a private garden, so what the community spends on the common parts matters more to a letting owner here than it would inland.

The counterweight is the arcade, and it should be faced honestly before the first booking. Frontline blocks sit above bars and restaurants whose licences and hours are set by the Ayuntamiento de Manilva, not by your building, and the owners who live here year-round already know exactly how that sounds in July. When a comunidad de propietarios is asked to approve tourist letting, that is the argument in the room. A flat two rows back is a quieter proposition and a slower one; a frontline flat earns more weeks and generates more correspondence. Choose which of the two you are actually running, and let the guest description say so plainly.

The Duquesa year, and the licence layers in Manilva

The year has an unusual tail. Midsummer is the obvious part, and the arcade fills every evening. What follows it is not silence: the operator reports more than 600 owners keeping this basin as their usual mooring, and boats bring their owners back for lifting out, painting and servicing long after the beach towns have shut. Those visitors want a serviced flat for a week in November rather than a family apartment for a fortnight in August, and an owner who never advertises for them leaves the quietest part of the calendar empty.

The regulatory picture, as at 26 August 2026, is three layers and they are routinely confused. Regionally, a vivienda con fines turísticos is set up by declaración responsable and registered in the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía under Decreto 28/2016. Nationally, since 3 April 2025 the Ley de Propiedad Horizontal requires the community's express prior approval and allows a three-fifths majority to limit or condition tourist letting. Municipally, the authority here is the Ayuntamiento de Manilva. Confirm all three in writing on the day you act: this is live ground and last season's answer is not guidance.

How this runs, step by step

  1. Step 1

    Find the arcade in the minutes

    Specific to Manilva

    Before you commit to a letting plan, read three years of the block's minutes specifically for two things: how the community has handled noise from the units below, and whether tourist letting has ever been put to a vote. Those two threads usually run together at this address. A block that has already argued the question will tell you, in its own words, how a future vote is likely to go.

  2. Step 2

    Declare the home to the Junta

    As at 26 August 2026, a vivienda con fines turísticos in Andalucía is set up by declaración responsable and entered in the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía under Decreto 28/2016. The declaration is regional and it is the layer that most owners think is the whole answer. It is not: it says nothing about whether your particular building permits the activity, and it does not override a municipal restriction.

    ↗

  3. Step 3

    Confirm Manilva's position in writing

    Specific to Manilva

    The municipal layer for this address is the Ayuntamiento de Manilva at Calle Mar 34, and not any of the Cádiz town halls a few minutes west along the coast road. Municipal positions on tourist letting have moved more than once on this coast, so ask for Manilva's current position in writing on the date you act, and treat anything you were told last season as history rather than as guidance.

    ↗

  4. Step 4

    Minute the community's approval

    Since 3 April 2025 the Ley de Propiedad Horizontal has required an owner to obtain the community's express prior approval before letting to tourists, and it allows a three-fifths majority to limit or condition the activity. Get the decision minuted rather than assumed, and get it before you agree a purchase price on the strength of a letting plan. This is the layer that most often decides the outcome here.

    ↗

  5. Step 5

    Set the flat up for a walked arrival

    Guests here leave the car in the garage on the first evening and rarely move it. Specify accordingly: secure parking documented in the listing, a key handover that works after a late flight, somewhere for wet towels and a folding trolley for the quay. Blackout in the bedrooms is not a luxury in a frontline block, it is the difference between a good review and a complaint about the terrace below.

  6. Step 6

    Re-check both layers each season

    Regional requirements and municipal positions both move, and a letting operation that was compliant two seasons ago can quietly stop being so. Diary an annual check of the Andalucían registration requirements, of Manilva's current municipal position and of the latest minutes from your own community. Paula's standing advice on this ground is conservative: confirm before you commit, and never rely on what applied last year.

A flat here earns its strongest weeks in midsummer, when the arcade is full and the basin is busy, and it holds a thinner but real tail through the golf months and the winter maintenance season. That tail is what distinguishes this address from a purely seasonal one, and it is worth advertising for. Against income sit the community charge, the IBI set by Manilva, cleaning and changeovers, and management. Because Duquesa sits in the corridor's entry band, those running costs weigh proportionally harder than they would at the Sotogrande end. Ask Paula which row and which floor a specific letting plan actually needs.

Questions about this in Puerto de la Duquesa

Can I let a flat above the restaurant arcade?

Legally it turns on the same three layers as any flat here, but practically the arcade changes the politics inside your building. Owners who live above bars year-round tend to arrive at the letting vote with a settled view, and since April 2025 the community's express prior approval is required before you let to tourists at all. Read the minutes before you buy, and never treat a frontline flat's letting potential as automatic.

↗

What do winter guests actually come to Duquesa for?

Mostly for their boats. More than 600 owners keep this basin as their habitual mooring, and the port has a yard, a crane and a seventy-tonne travel lift, so lifting out, antifouling and engine work happen in the months nobody markets. Those guests book short, book late and care about heating, a working desk and a reliable connection rather than about a sea view. A flat set up for them fills weeks a summer listing never touches.

↗

Which town hall decides short lets at Duquesa?

The Ayuntamiento de Manilva, in Málaga province. That matters because the provincial boundary is only a few minutes west along the coast road, and owners here routinely read advice written for the Cádiz municipalities up the corridor and assume it applies. It does not. As at 26 August 2026 the municipal layer for this address is Manilva's alone, and its current position should be confirmed in writing on the day you rely on it.
