Riviera del Sol: the Miraflores slope between Calahonda and La Cala

Riviera del Sol is a hillside residential area of Mijas Costa, on the Málaga coast between Calahonda and La Cala de Mijas. It is built around Miraflores Golf, an eighteen-hole course opened in 1990, with terraced houses and low apartment blocks stepping down towards Playa Riviera and the A-7.

Riviera del Sol occupies the slope between the A-7 and the low ridge that carries Miraflores Golf, roughly halfway along the Mijas coastline. It belongs to the coast's second wave of building rather than its first: laid out as a resort-scale scheme with its own course, its own commercial parades and its own road network, then filled in over three decades. The Ayuntamiento de Mijas lists the beach below it, Playa Riviera, within the Calahonda coastal zone, which tells you how closely the two addresses are stitched together on the ground.

The stock is dominated by terraced houses and low apartment blocks arranged in gated communities of a few dozen units each, most of them built from the late 1980s through to the mid-2000s. Roofs are pitched and tiled, walls are white, and almost every unit has a terrace angled at the sea. Detached villas exist, generally higher up towards the Miraflores clubhouse, and they are the exception rather than the rule. Because the communities were built in sequence rather than all at once, two neighbouring streets can differ sharply in layout, ceiling height and how much parking came with the deeds.

Daily life runs on a car and on the commercial parades near the A-7, where the supermarkets, the pharmacy and a row of restaurants sit within a short drive of most of the hill. Miraflores Golf is central to the social life of the area in a way a course rarely is, the club presents itself as a social club that happens to own eighteen holes, and the adjoining sports village adds an academy range and a training centre. Fuengirola and La Cala de Mijas cover the weekly shop, the school run and a proper Saturday market.

The honest caveat is the gradient and the motorway. From the upper streets the beach is a pleasant walk down and a long one back, and the A-7 sits between the housing and the sand; the crossings exist, but the road is audible from parts of the hill. Anyone sensitive to traffic noise or to winter shade should stand on the specific terrace, not just tour the urbanisation. The market read is that Riviera del Sol trades on the amount of house you get and on the golf, not on a walkable town centre, and that is exactly who keeps buying here.

Two eras dominate. The late 1980s and 1990s produced terraces and duplexes in an Andalusian idiom, white render, barrel-tiled roofs, arched openings, modest windows, clustered around shared pools on stepped plots. The 2000s brought larger blocks with underground parking, lifts and bigger glazing, generally lower down towards the A-7. Detached villas sit above the course and range from restrained 1990s builds to individually commissioned houses. Renovation stock is plentiful: many of the earliest units have never been touched beyond a kitchen, and the tiled open terraces are the obvious place to add value.

February is quiet here but not shuttered: the supermarkets, the golf club restaurant and a handful of the parade bars stay open, while the beach chiringuitos keep seasonal hours. Most residents eat either at the club or down on the Calahonda and La Cala seafronts, both a short drive. The school run goes to Fuengirola, La Cala or the international schools further west, which sets the rhythm of the morning queue at the A-7 slip roads. The prevailing sound is birdsong and a low motorway hum, and in July, other people's pools.

It suits buyers who want a house rather than a flat, want a golf course they can reach on foot, and accept that a car is part of the arrangement: Northern European second-home owners and retirees have been the steady pool here for thirty years. It does not suit anyone who wants to live without driving, anyone who wants an old Spanish town centre on the doorstep, or anyone who needs silence: the A-7 is close, and the upper slopes make the beach a one-way walk.

What moves the price here

On the Mijas ladder Riviera del Sol sits below Calahonda and well below La Cala de Mijas, and above Mijas Pueblo and Las Lagunas de Mijas. The gap with Calahonda is narrow and comes down to frontage: how much of each urbanisation genuinely reaches the beach road. Against the inland districts the difference is simply the sea, the golf and the second-home demand that follows both.

Height on the slope and what that height buys in view, distance from the A-7 and its noise, the age and specification of the community, whether parking is underground or a shared forecourt, and how recently the terrace, the kitchen and the bathrooms were rebuilt.

Questions about Riviera del Sol

Can residents of Riviera del Sol play Miraflores Golf freely?

No. Miraflores Golf is a members' club with its own share capital, and living on the slope gives no playing right by itself. Visitors book green fees like anywhere else, and members hold shares: the club's own history records a buy-out in 1998 in which members financed the purchase of a block of shares. If regular play matters to you, ask about membership terms and any waiting list before you sign anything, because that answer changes the cost of living here more than the community fee does.

↗

Which beach sits directly below Riviera del Sol?

Playa Riviera, which the Ayuntamiento de Mijas files under its Calahonda coastal zone rather than as a separate stretch. That municipal grouping is a fair description of what you see on the ground: the sand runs continuously past Calahonda, Riviera and on towards El Chaparral, with the A-7 above it and pedestrian crossings at intervals. Mijas reports its quality certifications and Blue Flags at municipal level, so check the current season's list for the specific stretch you plan to use.

↗

Is Riviera del Sol simply part of Calahonda, or separate?

They are separate schemes that touch. Calahonda is governed by its own conservation entity with obligatory membership; Riviera del Sol is a later, smaller development organised around Miraflores Golf, with its own communities and its own commercial parades. In everyday speech and on portals the two names blur, and the Ayuntamiento even files the Riviera beach in the Calahonda zone. For a purchase the distinction matters, because the charges, the governing body and the road access are not the same on both sides of the boundary.

How noticeable is A-7 traffic noise on the hillside here?

It varies enormously street by street, which is why the answer has to be given on the terrace rather than on a map. The A-7 runs along the foot of the area, so the lower rows hear it most, but sound also carries upwards on still evenings and is reflected by the slope in places. The tolled AP-7 takes some of the summer through-traffic away from it. Visit twice, once in the middle of a weekday morning, once after dark, before deciding a particular unit is quiet.

Are these terraced houses big enough to live in permanently?

Many are, and a substantial number of residents do exactly that. The typical scheme gives you two or three bedrooms over two floors, a solarium and a small private garden or patio, which works well for a couple or a couple with visiting family. What catches people out is storage, winter heating and how much of the terrace is genuinely usable when the wind gets up. Older units also tend to have small windows, so daylight in the ground-floor living room is worth checking in January rather than in June.
