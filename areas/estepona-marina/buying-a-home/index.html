Estepona Marina & Port · Buying a home

Buying at Estepona port, where the boatyard sets the view

Estepona's port is a finished district on 129,889 square metres of land, so buying here is not a choice between urbanisations but between arcs of one basin. A flat over the varadero, over the fishing pier, over the restaurant quay or facing open water past the Faro de Punta Doncella are four different homes.

Start with a compass rather than a floor plan. The whole port service zone covers 284,107 square metres and only 129,889 of that is land, so nearly every apartment here looks at the same water from a different angle, and the angle is the purchase. The varadero is 4,200 square metres of working boatyard: hulls out of the water, sanders running, antifoul going on. It is interesting for a month and wearing for a decade. One arc round, the 406-metre fishing pier moves boxes before dawn. One arc further, the restaurant line closes after midnight. No listing tells you which of those a flat faces, so you have to establish it yourself.

Then read the deed, because the quay itself is not for sale. The basin and its quays are public port domain, administered by the Agencia Pública de Puertos de Andalucía and operated under concession by Marinas del Mediterráneo and the Club Náutico de Estepona. The chandlers and restaurants you walk past hold a right of use with an end date, not a freehold. What is genuinely on the market is the land behind them: four- to six-storey blocks from the 1970s and 1980s, plus later infill towards Playa del Cristo. Inside that stock the honest checklist is short: lift, garage, which arc, and whether the terrace on the registered plan is the terrace you walked onto.

What comes to market around the basin

Almost everything here is an apartment, and almost all of it dates from the two decades when the port was built out. Blocks of four to six storeys with deep balconies and garages underneath make up the bulk; behind and beside them sit later infill and a small number of newer buildings towards Playa del Cristo with glass balustrades and a roof pool. Detached houses barely exist. That uniformity is useful, because within one block you can often compare three flats sharing a plan, so floor, arc and condition carry the whole difference. The renovation stock is the untouched original: sound concrete, 1980s bathrooms, single glazing facing salt air.

You are usually bidding against someone who already knows the port. Boat owners who hold a berth under concession from the Agencia Pública de Puertos de Andalucía and are tired of driving to it. Retired Northern European couples who want dinner without a car. Spanish buyers from the town itself, better represented here than on the coastal urbanisations. Investors buying to let are a fourth group and a smaller one, because blocks differ in how they feel about short stays. The practical consequence is that a good flat rarely reaches a portal in a neglected state: the neighbours hear about it first, and Paula's read is that patience and a standing brief beat a weekend of viewings.

How this runs, step by step

  1. Step 1

    Fix which arc you are buying

    Specific to Estepona

    The basin has four working faces and they are not interchangeable. The varadero covers 4,200 square metres and is a yard rather than a view: hulls come out, sanders run, antifoul is applied. The 406-metre fishing pier moves boxes before dawn. The restaurant line closes after midnight. Open water past the lighthouse is the quietest and the dearest. Settle the face before you shortlist, not after.

    ↗

  2. Step 2

    Compare the terrace with the Catastro

    Balconies on these blocks were often glazed in at some point in the last forty years, and an enclosure that never reached the Catastro record becomes the buyer's problem rather than the seller's. Have your lawyer put the registered plan beside the flat you actually walked through. Where they differ, an architect prices legalising or reversing it, and that number belongs in your offer rather than in your third winter.

  3. Step 3

    Read three years of salt costs

    Specific to Estepona

    A front-line building on this basin spends money an inland block never sees: render, railings, lift machinery and anything else standing in salt air. Ask the administrator for three years of accounts and the reserve position before you accept the current fee as normal, and ask specifically what has been deferred. Deferred is not saved. It is invoiced later, and by then it is invoiced to you.

  4. Step 4

    Walk to both central-zone beaches

    Specific to Estepona

    Estepona's tourism office counts exactly two beaches in its central zone. Playa del Cristo is the sheltered 680-metre cove five hundred metres from both ports, well protected from the wind and busy with families at weekends; Playa de La Rada is the long town beach the other way. Walk both from the front door of the flat you are considering, in the shoes you would actually wear. That walk is most of what a harbour address is worth.

    ↗

  5. Step 5

    Nota simple, arras and notary

    From the offer onwards the mechanics are national rather than local. Your lawyer takes a nota simple from the Registro de la Propiedad to confirm ownership and any charge on the flat, the arras contract fixes the price and the completion date, and the escritura pública is signed before a notary and then registered. A harbour address changes none of that order. What it changes is the list of things worth checking before you reach it.

  6. Step 6

    Plan for the empty months

    A flat used in bursts still stands in salt air all year. Budget for someone to open windows, run the taps and check the terrace drains after an autumn storm, and decide before completion whether that is a neighbour, the administrator or a paid key-holder. Owners who arrange it in the first month rarely mention it again. Owners who arrange it after the first leak mention it for years.

On the Estepona ladder the harbour quarter sits above Estepona Old Town and Cancelada and below El Paraíso and the New Golden Mile, and at that level what you are buying is outlook rather than floor area. On top of the agreed figure, a resale carries Andalucía's transfer tax, ITP, while a flat sold new by its promoter is IVA plus AJD instead; Paula's lawyer confirms which applies, in writing, before you commit. Then budget the front-line running costs: a community fee that pays for salt, and a lift that will eventually need replacing. Ask Paula for comparables inside the same block rather than for the port.

Questions about this in Estepona Marina & Port

Which arc of the basin should I look at?

That depends on what you can tolerate rather than on what photographs best. The boatyard side is busy in daylight and dead at night. The fishing pier is loud before dawn and quiet by lunch. The restaurant quay is the reverse of both. Open water past the lighthouse is the calmest and is priced accordingly. Paula's habit is to walk a buyer round the basin twice, once at nine in the morning and once at eleven at night, before any flat goes on a shortlist.

Do these blocks have lifts and underground parking?

Most of them do, and that is the practical difference between this quarter and the old town two streets away. The blocks around the basin were built with garages at or below ground level, and lifts were standard from the start, which is why the harbour attracts buyers who have already tried carrying shopping up a staircase somewhere else. Not every building has both, though, and a parking space is sometimes a separate deed with its own price. Confirm both in the nota simple, not in the listing.

Is an enclosed terrace a problem when buying?

It can be, and it is common enough on this basin to be worth checking every time. Many balconies were glazed in during the decades after the blocks went up, and where that was never licensed or never reached the Catastro record, the discrepancy transfers to you with the keys. It is rarely fatal. It is a negotiation: your lawyer establishes what was permitted, an architect prices legalising or reversing it, and the figure goes into the offer rather than into your first winter.
