Letting in Coín, where the classification comes first

Letting in Coín starts with a legal question rather than a calendar one. Decreto 28/2016 confines the vivienda con fines turísticos regime to dwellings on land of residential use, and a large share of this municipality's lettable stock stands out in the campo. Where the house sits decides whether the regime reaches it at all.

The regime is narrower than most owners expect. Article 3 of Decreto 28/2016, de 2 de febrero, defines a vivienda con fines turísticos as one in a building situated on land of residential use, and article 1.2 expressly excludes rural dwellings governed by article 48 of Ley 13/2011 and by Decreto 20/2002, which belong to a separate rural-tourism regime altogether. In a municipality where thousands of residents live outside every registered nucleus, that single line does more to shape the letting question than any view of the market. Both provisions were read on 26 August 2026, and the position should be re-checked before you commit rather than relied on from a page.

The second gate is physical. Article 6.a requires the dwelling to hold a licencia de ocupación and to meet the technical and quality conditions demanded of housing, which is exactly where a Coín country house built in phases over three decades tends to stop. An owner who intends to let should therefore establish the occupation position before the purchase, not afterwards, because retrofitting it is a municipal procedure with municipal timing and no guaranteed outcome. Where the property is a flat inside a block, article 17.12 of the Ley de Propiedad Horizontal separately allows the community to approve, condition or prohibit tourist letting by a three-fifths majority.

The season in Coín, and the licence position in the municipality

The regulation first, because it is the part that dates. Andalusian tourist letting runs on Decreto 28/2016, de 2 de febrero: the regime covers dwellings on land of residential use, requires a licencia de ocupación and the technical conditions demanded of housing, is entered in the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía on a declaración responsable, and caps capacity at fifteen guests for a whole dwelling. Rural dwellings are excluded and sit under a separate regime. Every one of those provisions was read on 26 August 2026.

What no page should tell you is what the municipality will grant. Municipal positions on tourist letting differ along this coast and are being actively revised, so the only safe instruction is to confirm the Ayuntamiento de Coín's position in writing, for your specific address, as at the day you commit, never to rely on a sentence written weeks earlier. Where letting is part of the plan, Paula treats that written confirmation as a condition of the purchase.

The season is the least dramatic part. There is no beach week here and no resort calendar, so short-let demand is thin and largely tied to the valley in spring and early autumn. The market that actually pays every month is residential and year-round. Owners who arrive expecting a coastal summer and find a working town usually end up letting long, and doing rather better than they expected.

How this runs, step by step

  1. Step 1

    Establish which regime reaches the house

    Specific to Coín

    Before anything else, have your lawyer state in writing whether the property sits on land of residential use, which brings it inside Decreto 28/2016, or on rustic land, which does not. Article 1.2 excludes rural dwellings from this decree entirely. In this municipality that answer differs street by street and is not something an agent's listing can settle for you.

    ↗

  2. Step 2

    Confirm the occupation licence exists

    Specific to Coín

    Article 6.a of the decree requires a licencia de ocupación and continued compliance with the technical and quality conditions demanded of housing. A Coín country house assembled over several phases frequently has no clean occupation record for the building as it now stands, and no amount of furnishing changes that. Establish the position with the Ayuntamiento de Coín in writing, before purchase.

    ↗

  3. Step 3

    Check the community where there is one

    For a flat in one of the town's blocks, article 17.12 of the Ley de Propiedad Horizontal lets the owners approve, condition or prohibit tourist letting by three-fifths of owners and three-fifths of participation quotas. Read the minutes and the estatutos before you buy, not after. A detached house in the campo has no comunidad de propietarios and therefore no such vote.

    ↗

  4. Step 4

    File the declaración responsable

    Where the regime does apply, article 9 of the decree has the owner file a declaración responsable with the tourism authority, after which the dwelling is entered ex officio in the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía. Article 5.2 caps capacity at fifteen guests for a whole dwelling and four per bedroom, which on a large cortijo is a real constraint rather than a formality.

    ↗

  5. Step 5

    Choose the tenancy before the furniture

    Specific to Coín

    In Coín the dependable demand is residential and annual: households working in the valley, staff at the health centre and the schools, families waiting on a build. That market wants heating, storage, parking and a twelve-month contract, not a welcome basket. Deciding this before you furnish saves a second refit, because the two kinds of tenant want almost opposite things from the same house.

  6. Step 6

    Advertise with the registration number

    Once a dwelling is inscribed, the registration code belongs in every advertisement, on every platform, every time. The Junta operates a public reporting channel for unregistered tourist accommodation, so an omission is not a low-visibility administrative slip. Keep the certificate, the inscription and the code together in one file that a manager can produce without calling you.

    ↗

A house here earns like a home rather than like a holiday let: twelve steady months instead of a short, high season and a long empty one. That trades a lower peak for a fuller year and a far simpler operation: one contract, one changeover, no cleaning rota in August. The homes that let well are the ones with heating that works in a valley winter, storage, and parking a family can use, not the ones with the largest terrace. Ask Paula what comparable houses in the town and in the campo are genuinely achieving before you model anything at all.

Questions about this in Coín

Can I let a Coín campo house to holidaymakers?

Not under the ordinary vivienda con fines turísticos route, if the house stands on rustic land. Article 3 of Decreto 28/2016 confines that regime to dwellings on land of residential use, and article 1.2 expressly excludes rural dwellings, which fall under a separate rural-tourism framework with its own requirements. Read on 26 August 2026. So the first question is never the calendar or the platform: it is the classification of the ground the house stands on, confirmed in writing by your own lawyer.

↗

Does a long let make better sense in this town?

For most owners in Coín, yes, and for two reasons that reinforce each other. The demand that pays every month is residential: people who work in the valley, and families who need somewhere while a build finishes. And a long let sidesteps the whole occupation-licence and registration question that governs tourist use. The trade is a lower headline figure for a fuller year, one contract instead of fifty, and none of the changeover cost that makes a remote short-let property expensive to run.

Which tenants actually rent here all year round?

The people the town runs on. Coín has schools, a health centre and a full high street, so there is a standing population of teachers, health staff, tradespeople and administrative workers who need somewhere permanent rather than somewhere charming. Add families waiting out a renovation, and newcomers renting for a year before they buy: a pattern Paula actively encourages, because a winter here is the only honest test of whether an inland valley suits someone who has only ever visited the coast.
