Letting in Sotogrande Alto: a lease, not a licence

Most homes above the A-7 are let on an ordinary residential lease rather than to holidaymakers, because the tenant who wants ten months on this hillside already exists. That makes Sotogrande Alto the one part of the estate where the tourist-licence question is often the wrong question to start with.

Letting here starts with a choice between two different legal products, and most owners above the road make it by accident. A long residential lease is governed by the Ley de Arrendamientos Urbanos, needs no tourism registration, runs for months rather than nights and produces one set of keys and one contract. A tourist let is a registered activity with its own regime, its own advertising rules and, in a gated sub-community, its own politics. Both are legitimate. They are simply not the same business, and the furniture, the insurance, the management contract and the tax treatment all follow from which one you chose.

The second thing that separates the upper estate from the seaward half is that a good part of its stock sits inside gated schemes of townhouses and apartments, which the original ground does not have. Those are comunidades de propietarios, so the neighbours' vote on tourist letting genuinely applies: the exact opposite of a villa standing alone on its own plot. If you own in one, read the minutes before you furnish anything. If you own a detached house on a lettered-zone plot, that vote is not your constraint, and the practical limits are the drive to Almenara's three nine-hole circuits and how cold the hillside gets in January.

The season above the A-7, and the licence position in San Roque

The upper estate does not really have a season in the coastal sense. Its calendar is an academic year with golf around the edges: families in residence from September to June, visiting members and tournament weeks in spring and autumn, and a high summer that matters less here than it does two kilometres down the hill. That shape is why the long lease is the natural product above the road and the short let is the exception rather than the default.

Where an owner does want to let short, three things have to line up, and as at 26 August 2026 all three are moving. Regionally, Decreto 28/2016 governs the tourist home and registration runs through the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía; the Junta's registry pages now describe these as viviendas de uso turístico and publish guidance on adapting homes that were already registered, which is a signal that requirements have shifted rather than settled. Nationally, the community's express prior approval is required and a three-fifths vote can limit or condition letting, which bites on a gated townhouse scheme above the road and not on a detached villa. Municipally, San Roque is the authority, and its position should be obtained in writing on the day you act. Paula's standing advice is to treat all three as a position as at that date, never as a guarantee of what you will be granted.

How this runs, step by step

  1. Step 1

    Read your sub-community's minutes first

    Specific to San Roque

    If the house sits inside one of the gated schemes above the road, the comunidad de propietarios decides whether tourist letting happens at all. Spanish horizontal-property law requires its express prior approval and lets it limit or condition letting by a three-fifths vote. Pull the last three years of minutes before anything else. A detached house on its own lettered-zone plot sits outside that mechanism entirely.

    ↗

  2. Step 2

    Choose the lease or the licence

    A residential lease under the Ley de Arrendamientos Urbanos and a registered tourist home are separate products with separate obligations. Decide which one you are running before you buy a sofa, because the furniture, the insurance cover, the reporting duties and the management contract all differ. Mixing the two badly, or switching halfway through a year, is where most of the margin quietly disappears.

  3. Step 3

    Register, and check what has changed

    For a tourist let, Andalucía's regime sits in Decreto 28/2016 and registration runs through the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía. As at 26 August 2026 the Junta's own registry pages describe these homes as viviendas de uso turístico and publish guidance on adapting homes already entered in the register. Treat that as live ground and have the lawyer confirm the current requirement rather than a remembered one.

    ↗

  4. Step 4

    Ask San Roque, in writing, this season

    Specific to San Roque

    The municipality is San Roque, and municipal positions on tourist letting along this coast have moved more than once. Ask what applies on the date you act and keep the written answer with the file. Paula's phrasing is deliberate: this is the municipality's position as at the day it was given, not a promise about what a future owner will be granted.

    ↗

  5. Step 5

    Furnish for a winter, not a fortnight

    Specific to San Roque

    A ten-month tenant arrives with school bags and stays through the cold months. That makes storage, a utility room, a desk and heating that actually works in January worth more than a show kitchen. The hillside sits under cork oak and holds damp; a house that is charming in August can be bleak in February. Check the boiler, the gutters and the leaf fall before the first tenancy, not during it.

  6. Step 6

    Decide who gets the golf weeks

    Specific to San Roque

    Spring and autumn bring golf demand around Valderrama and Almenara's twenty-seven holes, and those are the weeks an owner is most tempted to keep. A long lease removes them entirely: the house is the tenant's and you are a guest in it. Settle that before the contract is drawn rather than discovering it when a tournament week comes round and the keys are not yours.

    ↗

A ten-month lease on this hillside trades a lower weekly rent for far more weeks of it, and it produces a flatter, more predictable line than anything the shoreline offers. A tourist let does the reverse and competes against homes a guest can walk from, which the upper estate cannot offer. Against either sit costs the coast does not carry: a wooded plot's gardener, a pool through the winter, real heating in January, the estate conservation charge and, in a gated scheme, a second charge below it. Paula prices the two products separately rather than blending them into one expectation.

Questions about this in Sotogrande Alto

Do I need a tourist licence for a ten-month tenancy?

No. A lease of that length is a residential tenancy under the Ley de Arrendamientos Urbanos, not a tourism activity, so the Andalucían tourist-home register is the wrong door. What it does need is a properly drafted contract, a deposit lodged as the law requires and a clear position on utilities and garden maintenance. Owners above the road sometimes register out of caution and then find the registration commits them to obligations a residential lease never triggers. Ask the lawyer which product you are actually running.

Does the neighbours' vote apply to a detached Alto villa?

Not in the same way. The three-fifths mechanism runs through horizontal-property law, which governs buildings and schemes divided into separate units. A villa standing alone on a lettered-zone plot is not part of one, so its owner has no community to persuade. A townhouse or apartment inside one of the gated schemes above the road is, and there the vote is decisive. That single distinction is why letting advice written for the estate cannot be applied to the whole of it.

↗

Is heating a real cost on the upper estate?

Yes, and it surprises owners who bought in July. The hillside sits higher than the shoreline and under a cork-oak canopy that keeps the sun off, so a house here is genuinely cooler, which is the point in August and a cost from December to March. A tenant living through the academic year will use the heating, and a house without a working system will be handed back. Check the boiler, the insulation and the gutters before the first tenancy rather than after the first complaint.
